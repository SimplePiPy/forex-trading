The Hook Bank

120 hooks for tradesmen who want strangers to stop scrolling. Fill in the brackets, film the first frame, post.

From the 90-Day Speedrun Playbook · Version 1 · Sunday, September 13, 2026

How to use this

A hook is the first two seconds. It's the text on screen plus what the camera shows plus, if you're talking, the first sentence out of your mouth. All three land at once.

Three rules before you pick one:

  1. The first frame is never your logo, a slate, or you saying "hey guys." It's the most extreme thing in the video, or the hook text on a clean frame.
  2. One hook, one idea, one post. If the hook needs a second sentence to make sense, it's a caption, not a hook.
  3. Say it, then show it. The visual should pay off the text within 3 seconds. "The moment the slab lets go" cuts to the slab letting go, not to you explaining the slab.

Every hook below has a fill-in-the-blank version and one worked example from a real trade. Swap in yours. Brackets are yours to fill.

Test any hook with three questions: Would a stranger stop? Can I show it in 3 seconds? Is there one idea? Three yeses, film it.

1 · The Moment (extreme visual, the thing they can't look away from)

Use when you have footage that's genuinely wild. The text just labels it.

  1. The moment a [heavy thing] lets go. Tree: The moment a 2,000 lb limb lets go, 60 feet up.
  2. [Number] [units] of [material], one [tool]. Concrete: 14 yards of concrete, one screed.
  3. [Distance] from [the thing you can't hit]. No room for a mistake. Electrician: 8 inches from a live 480. No room for a mistake.
  4. What [X] pounds of [material] sounds like when it [lands / cracks / starts]. Roofer: What 900 pounds of shingles sounds like when it hits the dump trailer.
  5. POV: you get paid to do this. Welder: POV: you get paid to do this. (arc on, close, no talking)
  6. This is what $[X] looks like from [here]. Tree: This is what $40,000 looks like from the top.
  7. [Start] to [finish] in [seconds] seconds. Framer: Slab to roof in 40 seconds.
  8. The [quiet part of your job], no music. Painter: Cutting in a ceiling line, no music.
  9. [Number] seconds that decide whether you go home. Tree: The 3 seconds that decide whether you go home.
  10. Nobody tells you the [material] talks. Listen at [timestamp]. Tree: Nobody tells you the tree talks. Listen at 0:08.
  11. Watch the [thing] at [timestamp]. Mechanic: Watch the timing chain at 0:04.
  12. The part of the job the customer never sees. Plumber: The part of the job the customer never sees. (crawlspace, headlamp)

2 · The Open Loop (something happened, and you have to watch to find out)

Use when the footage has an ending. Never give the ending away in the text.

  1. They said this [job] couldn't be done. Watch. Roofer: Three companies said this roof couldn't be saved. Watch.
  2. I wasn't going to post this one. Any trade. Use on your best old footage with a new first frame.
  3. The [job] I still think about. Tree: The cut I still think about.
  4. The [job] that made me want to quit for a day. HVAC: The attic that made me want to quit for a day.
  5. The moment the [material] tells you it's going the wrong way. Tree: The moment the tree tells you it's going the wrong way.
  6. I've done [number] of these. This one was different. Concrete: I've poured 400 driveways. This one was different.
  7. Something's wrong with this [job] and it took me [time] to see it. Electrician: Something's wrong with this panel and it took me 20 minutes to see it.
  8. The customer said "[quote]." Here's what happened. Landscaper: The customer said "just make it look nice." Here's what happened.
  9. This should've taken [time]. It took [longer time]. Here's why. Plumber: This should've taken an hour. It took nine. Here's why.
  10. I almost walked off this job at [time]. Roofer: I almost walked off this job at 2 PM.
  11. Everything went right until [timestamp]. Tree: Everything went right until 0:11.
  12. You'll see it before I did. Mechanic: You'll see it before I did. (the leak, the crack, the bad wire)

3 · Money (the number nobody in your trade says out loud)

Use for talking-head or breakdown posts. Real numbers only. Made-up numbers get found out.

  1. This is a $[X] [job]. Here's how a [customer] ends up paying that. Tree: This is a $40,000 tree job. Here's how a mobile home park ends up paying that.
  2. $[X] job. Here's where every dollar actually goes. Concrete: $18,000 driveway. Here's where every dollar actually goes.
  3. I turned down a $[X] job. Here's the math. Painter: I turned down a $12,000 job. Here's the math.
  4. The $[small number] tool that saves me $[big number] a month. HVAC: The $200 tool that saves me $2,000 a month.
  5. How I got a [customer] to pay a $[X] deposit before I touched anything. Tree: How I got a mobile home park to pay a $20,000 deposit before I touched a tree.
  6. This [job] costs $[X]. Here's why it's not $[lower X]. Electrician: This panel swap costs $4,500. Here's why it's not $2,000.
  7. I made $[X] on this job. I kept $[Y]. Landscaper: I made $9,000 on this job. I kept $1,400.
  8. The day rate that makes a [crew role] stay 3 years. Tree: The day rate that makes a groundie stay 3 years.
  9. How I'd start a [trade] company with $[X] today. Plumber: How I'd start a plumbing company with $10K today.
  10. The mistake that cost me $[X]. Don't do this. Roofer: The mistake that cost me $6,000. Don't do this.
  11. What a $[X] [job] and a $[Y] [job] have in common. Concrete: What a $3,000 patio and a $30,000 foundation have in common.
  12. I raised my prices [X]% and lost [Y] customers. Here's what happened to revenue. Any trade. Real numbers.

4 · The Confession (the thing you'd only tell another tradesman)

Use for trust. These build the audience that buys.

  1. The [job] I got wrong and what it cost me. Welder: The weld I got wrong and what it cost me.
  2. I didn't know this for [number] years. Mechanic: I didn't know this for 8 years.
  3. Nobody taught me this. I learned it the expensive way. Framer: Nobody taught me this. I learned it the expensive way.
  4. I used to [bad habit]. Here's when I stopped. Tree: I used to climb tired. Here's when I stopped.
  5. The call I'm embarrassed to say I took. Plumber: The call I'm embarrassed to say I took.
  6. I've been doing this wrong for [number] years. Painter: I've been rolling walls wrong for 12 years.
  7. If I'd known this at [age], I'd be [outcome] by now. Any trade: If I'd known this at 22, I'd be retired by now.
  8. The thing about this job I'm not supposed to say. Roofer: The thing about roofing I'm not supposed to say.
  9. My worst day in [trade]. Tree: My worst day in a tree.
  10. I got scared on this one. Electrician: I got scared on this one. (the arc flash close call)
  11. I'm posting this so you don't do it. HVAC: I'm posting this so you don't do it. (the unit somebody else installed)
  12. I lost a [customer / crew member / friend] over this. Any trade. Only if it's true and you're ready.

5 · The Callout (tag someone, send it to someone)

Use to earn shares. Shares are weighted heavier than likes.

  1. Tag the guy who'd do this with you. Tree: Tag the guy who'd do this with you.
  2. Tag your [crew role]. Tree: Tag your groundie. · Roofer: Tag your tear-off guy.
  3. Send this to the [person] who thinks [trade] is easy. Concrete: Send this to the guy who thinks concrete is easy.
  4. If you're a [trade], stop scrolling. Plumber: If you're a plumber, stop scrolling.
  5. Every [trade] has one of these. Mechanic: Every mechanic has one of these. (the customer, the tool, the car)
  6. If your [boss / customer / crew] does this, show them this. Framer: If your GC does this, show him this.
  7. You're not a real [trade] until [thing]. Welder: You're not a real welder until you've burned through your favorite shirt.
  8. Send this to the [family member] who asks what you do all day. Any trade: Send this to the wife who asks what you do all day.
  9. Every apprentice needs to see this. Electrician: Every apprentice needs to see this.
  10. The [trade] guys will get this. Nobody else will. HVAC: The HVAC guys will get this. Nobody else will.
  11. Show this to the guy who bid it lower. Roofer: Show this to the guy who bid it lower. (the roof he did, failing)
  12. Tag a [trade] who needs to hear this today. Any trade. Pair with the Confession or the Quiet.

6 · The Contrast (two things that don't belong together)

Use for the story of your life, not just your job.

  1. [Loud thing] yesterday. [Quiet thing] today. Tree: 70 feet up yesterday. Ocean today.
  2. [Time] AM: [the job]. [Time] PM: [the life]. Concrete: 5 AM pour. 5 PM with my kids.
  3. This is what [trade] looks like. This is what it pays for. Landscaper: This is what landscaping looks like. This is what it pays for. (the truck, the house, the kid)
  4. Same [job]. [Number] years apart. Painter: Same house. 11 years apart.
  5. The job vs the invoice. Plumber: The job vs the invoice. (the crawlspace, then the number)
  6. What they see vs what I see. Tree: What they see vs what I see. (a tree, then the rigging plan drawn over it)
  7. Day 1 vs Day [X]. Any trade running a counter.
  8. Me at [age] vs me now, same [tool]. Welder: Me at 19 vs me now, same hood.
  9. The bid vs the reality. Roofer: The bid vs the reality. (the "simple" roof, then the rot)
  10. [Trade] at 6 AM vs [trade] at 6 PM. Framer: Framing at 6 AM vs framing at 6 PM.
  11. What the customer paid for vs what they got. Any trade. Only when they got more.
  12. A desk job vs this. (no words, just the view) Tree: A desk job vs this. (the canopy, the sunrise)

7 · The Crew (the people nobody films)

Use for reach inside your trade and for shares. Crews share crew posts.

  1. Everybody watches the [lead role]. Nobody watches the guys who keep him alive. Tree: Everybody watches the climber. Nobody watches the guys who keep him alive.
  2. POV: you're the [crew role] and the [lead] just yelled "[call]." Tree: POV: you're the groundie and the climber just yelled "headache."
  3. Meet [name]. [Number] years with me. Here's what he taught me. Any trade. Say the name. Look at him.
  4. The [crew role] who's never been on camera. Concrete: The finisher who's never been on camera.
  5. What a good [crew role] does that nobody notices. Roofer: What a good ground man does that nobody notices.
  6. I'd trust him with my life. Here's why. Tree, roofer, electrician. Anywhere it's literally true.
  7. This is what a crew looks like when nobody's slacking. Landscaper: This is what a crew looks like when nobody's slacking.
  8. He started at [age] and didn't know how to [basic thing]. Watch him now. Welder: He started at 18 and couldn't strike an arc. Watch him now.
  9. The [crew role] of the week. Any trade. Make it a series.
  10. What it takes to keep 4 guys busy for a year. Any trade. Talking head, crew behind you.
  11. The hardest part of running a crew isn't the work. Any trade. Open on the guys, not you.
  12. He quit today. Here's what I told him. Any trade. Only if true.

8 · The Numbers (proof, growth, the counter)

Use when you're documenting a run in public. The screenshot is the visual.

  1. Day [X]. Here's exactly what happened. Day 2. I posted 3 times yesterday. Here's exactly what happened.
  2. Week [X] of [total]: [followers], [views], and the one thing I'd change. Week 1 of 90: 2,300 followers, 410K views, and the one thing I'd change.
  3. The hook that got [big number] vs the one that got [small number]. Same [job]. Same day. The hook that got 40K vs the one that got 800. Same tree. Same day.
  4. My best post got [X]. I'm posting it again with a different hook. Watch what happens. Then post the re-hook the next day and report.
  5. The post that flopped and what it taught me. Show the bad number. People trust the flop.
  6. My follower graph. Every dip and spike explained. Screen recording of Insights.
  7. I've tested [number] hooks. These [few] are the only ones that matter. I've tested 66 hooks. These 5 are the only ones that matter now.
  8. I'm [X] from [goal]. Here's the honest math on whether I'll make it. I'm 220K from 500K. Here's the honest math.
  9. I replied to [number] comments this week. Here's why that's the growth hack nobody does. Say the number. Show the thread.
  10. [Number] [tradesmen] posted the same hook at the same time. Here's what happened. For the crew climb day.
  11. The exact posting schedule I'm running. Steal it. Show the table on screen.
  12. You don't need to go viral. You need to go viral once. Talking head, on the job.

9 · The Business (how the money and the customers actually work)

Use for buyers. These posts get saved by the people who'll hire you or pay to learn from you.

  1. How to quote a [job] in 3 minutes (the sheet I use). Tree: How to quote a tree job in 3 minutes. (screen recording)
  2. 3 questions I ask before I take a job. Saved me from [number] lawsuits. Any trade.
  3. How I pay a crew on a $[X] job without going broke before the check clears. Deposit, draw, progress payment. One sentence each.
  4. [Number] systems that let me leave my company for [days] days. 5 systems that let me leave my tree company for 8 days.
  5. The one-page schedule that runs my whole company. Show it.
  6. Why most [trade] guys stay broke (and it's not the work). Talking head. Be specific: no deposits, no follow-up, pricing on feel.
  7. How to fire a client. Any trade. Everybody wants this one.
  8. How I collect the final [X]% without a fight. The photo, the walkthrough, the invoice on the spot.
  9. The insurance conversation every [trade] avoids. Any trade.
  10. My weekly money meeting, in 60 seconds. Film it. Friday. Truck.
  11. The email that lands $[X] jobs. Show the email. Read it.
  12. How I'd spend $[X] on a [trade] company right now. Any trade. Rank the spend.

10 · The Quiet (who you are when the tools are off)

Use sparingly, one a day at most, always real. This is why people stay.

  1. [Number] minutes of this before every [job]. Here's why. Tree: 10 minutes of this before every climb. (eyes closed, tailgate)
  2. Building this so I'm home when my kid is born, not on a job. Only if it's true. It's the most powerful hook in this document.
  3. The book that taught me more about running a crew than any business book. Hold it. Name it. One line.
  4. I write one page every morning. Here's the sentence from today. Notebook, coffee, read it.
  5. What I'm afraid of about [becoming a dad / the next 90 days / this job]. Talking head, quiet place.
  6. Close your eyes for 30 seconds. I'll count you in. Guided. Natural sound. Unusual enough to get tested as a Trial Reel.
  7. A [time] ago I was [where] with [nothing]. Same truck. Nothing changed. Everything changed. A month ago I was up a tree with 0 followers.
  8. The quiet part of a loud job. No words. The drive home, the sunset, the empty trailer.
  9. Focus is a muscle. Here's how I train it. Breath, eyes, the thing you do to get sharp. Keep the visual on you, not the tool.
  10. Why I'm only taking [small number]. For any offer. Because you're still doing the work every day.
  11. Thankful for a job that scares me. Holiday or milestone. Point of view, quiet.
  12. Day [X]. [Hospital / hotel / hospital parking lot]. Still posting. When it's true. That post will do five million.

The ten openers that kill a post

Delete the first two seconds if any of these are in them.

  1. "Hey guys"
  2. "So today we're going to…"
  3. Your logo
  4. A drone establishing shot with nothing happening
  5. "Welcome back to the channel"
  6. A slate or title card with music
  7. You walking to the camera
  8. "As you guys know…"
  9. "I get asked a lot…"
  10. A black frame or a fade-in
