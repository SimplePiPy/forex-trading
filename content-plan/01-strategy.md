# 01 — Strategy: The Big Idea, The Math, The Rules

## 1. The Big Idea

**"500K From The Treetops."**
A 90-day speedrun to 500,000 followers, documented in public, from the top of a tree.

Why this works when "just post good content" doesn't:

- **It has a clock.** Day counters make people come back. A speedrun is a story with a beginning, a middle and a deadline. Nobody follows a random tree guy every day. Everybody follows "Day 47 of 90."
- **It has stakes people can feel.** A $40,000 job you have to land safely. A vacation you're refusing to skip because you built a business that runs. And a second act nobody sees coming: the baby, revealed on Day 14 after you've told your parents at the beach. Those three facts alone are hook material for 90 days.

**The embargo:** no mention of the pregnancy anywhere public (Reels, captions, Stories, DMs, the Playbook, the bio) until you have told your parents in person at Oak Island. Days 0–13 run on the job, the speedrun, and the beach. The reveal is a planned post, not a slip.
- **It's visual in a way talking heads never are.** A GoPro at 70 feet with a chainsaw is scroll-stopping without a single word. Most growth coaches film in a bedroom. You film in the canopy.
- **The proof and the product are the same thing.** Every follower you gain is a receipt. By Day 45 you don't have to *claim* you can grow an account, you have a graph.
- **It's a format other people can copy, and you're going to teach it.** That's the mentorship.

### Positioning (say this out loud until it's boring)

> "I'm a tree climber who runs a tree company. I'm speed-running to 500K followers in 90 days and showing every number. I teach tradesmen and owners how to turn their trade into a brand that brings in bigger jobs, better people, and a life they actually get to live."

Three words for the profile: **Climber. Operator. Teacher.**

### Who you're growing for (two audiences, one account)

| Audience | Why they follow | What they buy |
|---|---|---|
| **The Watcher** (general public, 80% of followers) | Tree POV is insane. The story is human. The stillness content is calming. | Nothing directly. They are the reach that makes the account credible and the algorithm happy. |
| **The Operator** (tradesmen, blue-collar owners, small-business people, 20% of followers, 95% of buyers) | "He's one of us and he figured out the internet." They want bigger jobs, a brand, a way out of the truck. | The Sprint ($1,500) and the Climb ($3,700). |

You never have to choose. The Climb posts bring Watchers. The Operator and Speedrun posts convert Operators. The Stillness posts keep both.

## 2. The Math (read this so the goal doesn't crush you)

500,000 in 90 days = **5,556 new followers per day, average.**

That does not come from steady daily growth. It comes from outliers.

- Well-hooked POV content converts roughly **0.5%–2% of viewers to followers**. Use 1%.
- 500K followers at 1% = **~50 million views** over 90 days.
- At 3 Reels/day = 270 Reels. Average needed: ~185K views per Reel.
- Reality: most Reels will do 3K–50K. The 500K comes from **10–20 Reels doing 1M–10M each**, plus the halo they throw on everything else.

So the game is:
1. **Maximize the number of quality shots** (volume, but every one with a real hook).
2. **Find the winners fast** (Trial Reels, 48-hour data).
3. **Double down ruthlessly** (remix, re-cut, sequel every winner within 72 hours).
4. **Convert the spikes** (a pinned comment, a keyword DM, and a follow-worthy profile ready before the spike hits).

Three outcomes, all of which change your life:

| Tier | Followers at Day 90 | What it means for the offer |
|---|---|---|
| **Floor** | 30K–60K | 3–6 Sprint/Climb clients a month if the funnel is built. $10K–$20K/month. |
| **Target** | 150K–250K | 10–20 clients a month. Waitlist. You choose who you work with. |
| **Speedrun** | 500K+ | You are the reference account for "tradesman turns brand." Cohorts sell out on a Story. |

Be honest in your content about this too. "I might not hit it. Here's what I'll do either way." That honesty is itself a hook.

## 3. Four Pillars (every post belongs to exactly one)

### Pillar 1 — THE CLIMB (reach) · ~40% of posts
Tree work POV. Rigging, cuts, the drop, the dangerous moments, the crew, the $40K job. Voice-over lessons on skill, fear, focus, money. Zero educational fluff, just the work and one idea.

### Pillar 2 — THE SPEEDRUN (authority) · ~25% of posts
"Day X" updates. The numbers on screen. What hit, what flopped, the hook that got 2M, the one that got 400. What you learned. Teach the exact thing you did. This is the content that makes people want to be taught by you.

### Pillar 3 — THE OPERATOR (buyers) · ~20% of posts
How the business runs. Quoting a $40K job. Paying a crew. Collecting. Why you can take a week off. Mistakes that cost you money. Systems. Fatherhood as a reason to build it right.

### Pillar 4 — THE STILLNESS (retention, humanity) · ~15% of posts
Meditation before a big climb. Yoga on the beach at Oak Island. What you're reading. What you're writing. Preparing to be a dad. The quiet side of a loud job. This is what makes people say "I don't know why I follow him but I'd never unfollow."

**About shooting content:** keep it off the main account during the speedrun. Meta allows educational firearms content organically but treats it as a restricted category, it can throttle distribution and it blocks monetization tools. You are trying to make one account go nuclear. Don't hand the algorithm a reason to sandbag you. If you want it, run a separate account after Day 90, or frame it purely as "focus and breath work at the range" with no firearm as the visual subject. (See sources in 07.)

## 5. The Rules (from what you've learned from Jun Yu and everyone else, boiled down)

1. **The hook is the whole game.** First 1–2 seconds, visual and text together. Spend half your effort here. If the first frame is you talking to camera, the hook is the text. If the first frame is a tree, the hook is the tree.
2. **One idea per post.** If you can't say it in one sentence, it's two posts.
3. **Make content for strangers, not followers.** Assume nobody knows you. Never say "as you guys know."
4. **Watch time and shares beat likes.** Build for the rewatch (loops, payoffs at the end) and for the send ("send this to the guy on your crew who…").
5. **Native everything.** No watermarks, no reposts, captions burned in, text safe-zoned, 9:16, shot for sound-off.
6. **Reply to every comment in the first hour.** Then reply to every comment again that night. Comments with replies are a retention signal and the replies are content ideas.
7. **Post the same idea three ways.** A winner gets a sequel, a remix, and a carousel version. You're not being repetitive, 97% of viewers didn't see the first one.
8. **Trial Reels are your lottery tickets.** Every day, one post goes out as a Trial Reel (non-followers only). If it hits, promote it to the main grid.
9. **Never break the day counter.** Even on the beach, even on the day the baby comes. "Day 61. Hospital. Still posting." That post will do 5 million.
10. **Sell with proof, not pressure.** The counter, the graph, the member wins. Ask for the DM, not the sale.

## 6. What "nothing the internet has seen before" actually means here

Most growth coaches sell a course. You are going to run a **shared audience.**

Every member of the Crew gets **collab-posted on your account** at least once (Instagram Collab posts appear on both profiles with combined reach). When you're at 200K, a collab post is worth more to a 900-follower roofer than the entire course. That's the reason people pay $3,700 and the reason they tell their friends. See 02 for the full mechanic.

The second thing: **you update the Playbook live, in public, every Sunday.** Followers watch the strategy evolve. The Playbook at Day 90 is a document 500K people watched get written. Nobody sells that.

## 7. Operational security (read before every travel post)

You are building a public local business account fast. Strangers arrive in volume and you cannot vet any of them. Two things are genuinely worth protecting: an empty house, and a shop full of portable, resaleable equipment. Saws, climbing gear, trailers and a chipper are a known target for people who steal from contractors specifically.

None of this costs you content. The travel story is core to the Operator pillar. You just change *when* and *how precisely* you say it.

**Never say publicly:**
- A departure date or a return date. Not "Saturday," not "the 19th," not "eight days."
- A countdown. "3 days till the beach" is the single worst version.
- Where you live, your street, your house number, your mailbox, or a recognizable view of your house exterior or your neighborhood.
- Where the equipment sleeps. No shop exteriors, no trailer parked at the house, no "everything's locked in the shop while I'm gone."
- Real-time "I'm 300 miles away right now."

**Say instead:**
- "I've got a trip coming up." "I'm out of town for a while." "I'm not home this week."
- After you're back: "Last week I was at the beach and still posted three times a day." Past tense is free and carries the exact same lesson.

**Post travel content on a one-day delay.** Shoot Sunday, post Monday. Nobody can tell. It removes the live "the house is empty right now" signal and changes nothing about how the content performs.

**Before any trip:**
- A neighbor or a crew member knows, has a key, and parks in the driveway once.
- Equipment is inside and locked, or better, at someone else's place.
- Check what your business listings publish. Many tree companies have a home address on a Google Business Profile, a state license record, or an old Facebook page. If your home address is the business address, that's the thing to fix, not the Instagram caption.

**The rule of thumb:** say it vague beforehand, say it specific afterward.
