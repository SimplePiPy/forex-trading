## 1 · The $30,000 (do this before anything else, 20 minutes)

Three days of silence on thirty thousand dollars is not a scheduling problem anymore, it's a collections problem. It's Sunday so you can't call, but Sunday is actually the best day to send the email, because it sits at the top of her inbox Monday at 8 AM instead of being buried under the weekend.

### The follow-up email

Keep it warm, keep it short, and make it impossible to file away without answering.

> Subject: Progress payment — [job name] — invoice [#]
>
> Hi Maryann,
>
> Following up on the progress payment for the [park name] job. Invoice [#] for $30,000 went over on [date], and I haven't had a confirmation yet.
>
> The work it covers is complete. [One line: X trees down, stumps ground, everything over the homes and the lines cleared.] Happy to send photos of anything you want to see.
>
> Two things I need from you so I can plan:
> 1. Confirmation you received the invoice.
> 2. The date it's scheduled to be paid.
>
> If it's easier, put me in touch directly with whoever handles AP and I'll work with them so it's not sitting on your desk.
>
> I'm finishing the remaining trees the week of the 28th and I'd like this squared away before we start that phase.
>
> Thanks,
> [Name] · [Phone]

That last line matters. It quietly links the money to the work continuing without being a threat.

### Also today, 10 minutes
- **Re-read the contract.** Find the payment terms clause and any late fee or interest provision. If it's there, you can reference it next week. If it isn't, that's a lesson for your next contract and a future post.
- **Look up North Carolina's mechanics lien deadline** and write the date on your calendar. You are nowhere near needing it and you should still know exactly when it expires. That's a real deadline you can't recover if you miss it.
- **Write Monday's plan:** call her at 8:30. No answer, call the office main line at 9:00 and ask for accounts payable by name. Don't leave two voicemails with the same person.

### The content version
Do not name her, the park, or the company. But "I'm owed thirty thousand dollars and it's been quiet for three days" is a post that every contractor in America will watch to the end. Save it for midweek, see Day 10.
