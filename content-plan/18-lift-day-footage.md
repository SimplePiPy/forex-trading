# Lift Day Footage · What To Do With It

Two piles: the lift footage, and the one funny clip. The funny one is worth more than all the lift footage combined, and here's why.

---

## 1 · The trailer clip · post this first

**Why it matters more than good climbing footage:** shares are weighted heavily in Reels distribution, roughly three to five times a like. Climbing footage gets watched. Funny footage gets *sent*. Every tree guy in the country has a groundie they want to send this to, and every one of those sends puts you in front of a stranger who is exactly your audience.

You have almost no humor in the plan so far. This fixes that in one post.

### Cut it short and raw

Eight to twelve seconds. Do not edit it beyond a trim. Natural sound only, the mini skid is the punchline's timing. No music.

Ideally the cut is: two seconds before the skid enters frame, the skid passes, one beat of nothing after. The beat after is what makes it funny. Don't cut on the action.

### Hook options, on-screen text

**My pick: "Find the climber."**
It's an instruction, so people look. Looking is watch time. Then the reveal lands and they watch it again to see if they missed anything. That's a loop with no effort from you.

Alternates if it doesn't fit the frame:
- "Hour 11."
- "The mini skid didn't even slow down."
- "Nobody checked on me. Not one person."

Pick whichever matches what's actually in the shot. If you're hard to spot at first, "Find the climber" wins. If you're obvious, "Nobody checked on me" is funnier.

### Caption

> Somewhere in this trailer is a certified arborist.
>
> Full saddle, spikes, still clipped in. Hour eleven of a day that started at seven. The crew stepped over me and kept working.
>
> Tag the guy on your crew who does this. 👇

### Pinned comment

> Serious question: what's the worst place you've fallen asleep on a jobsite? 👇

That comment section will be the best one you've had. Guys will post photos.

### One note

This is the visual punchline to the couch video you already posted about being wrecked. Same story, told funny, two days later. That's a callback, and callbacks are how people go from watching one post to following an account.

## 2 · The lift footage · this is your beach week bank

You now have the thing the plan needed: a pile of Slot A footage you can post from while you're away. Break it into five posts so you're not scrambling.

| Post | The moment | On-screen hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The lift rising, ground falling away, the reveal of the whole job from height | "The view from 60 feet, without climbing a single foot." |
| 2 | The best single cut from the basket, big piece coming off | "One cut. Watch what [X] hundred pounds does on the way down." |
| 3 | Working directly over a structure, the tight one | "Six inches of margin and a house underneath." |
| 4 | Lift vs climbing, talking head over lift B-roll | "I climb for a living. Here's when I refuse to." |
| 5 | The whole tree, start to finish, sped up | "This tree took [X] hours. Here it is in 30 seconds." |

**Post 4 is the sleeper.** Arborists will argue in your comments about when a lift is the right call versus climbing, and argument is reach. It's also genuinely useful. The honest answer is something like: dead wood you can't trust, no good tie-in point, a tree you can't rig off safely, or a day where the schedule matters more than your pride. Say the real reason.

**Post 5 needs almost no skill to edit.** Speed ramp the whole thing, cut the boring parts, put the runtime on screen. Those consistently perform.

## 3 · Do this before you get on the road

Twenty minutes, and it's the difference between a beach week that runs itself and a beach week where you're fighting your phone every morning.

1. **Dump every card** to the laptop and to Google Drive. Both. Cards can fail, and you will not be able to reshoot any of this.
2. **One text file**, `LiftDay_notes.txt`. Five lines, the five moments above, each with the card and rough timestamp. "Card 2, ~34:00, the big piece off the back."
3. **Rough-cut the trailer clip now** and post it before you leave, or schedule it.
4. **Rough-cut at least two of the five** so Monday and Tuesday are already in the can.
5. **Charge and clear everything** so you arrive with empty cards and full batteries.

If you only do one of these, do number one.

---

## 4 · Opsec check on this footage

Jobsite equipment is fine. Trailers, the mini skid, the lift, the chipper, all of it on a customer's property is normal content and tells nobody anything useful.

What stays off camera is where the gear sleeps. No shop exterior, no trailer parked at your house, no driveway with the equipment in it. And keep the vague language about the trip: "I'm out of town for a while," never a date.

---

## 5 · Posting order

1. **The trailer clip.** Today. It's the strongest thing you have and it's timely.
2. **Lift post 1**, the reveal, tomorrow.
3. Then 2 through 5 across the week, alternating with the beach Slot B and C content.

Save the "this tree took X hours" one for midweek. You'll want something with weight in it when the beach content starts feeling light.
