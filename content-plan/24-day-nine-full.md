### VIDEO B · Slot B · "How to quote a tree job in 3 minutes"
**52 seconds · Already shot Monday · The funnel post**

The template and the ManyChat flow are built. Run it today.

#### Timeline

| Time | Picture | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | **Screen recording of the quote sheet**, scrolling, numbers blurred. Hook text on. | VO starts immediately |
| 0:04 | Cut to your face | "Most guys quote off feeling" |
| 0:10 | Insert: the man hours section of the sheet, 2 s | continues |
| 0:18 | Insert: the access multiplier cell, 2 s | continues |
| 0:28 | Insert: the risk lines, 2 s | continues |
| 0:38 | Insert: the debris lines, 2 s | continues |
| 0:46 | Back to your face | "send it the same day" |
| 0:52 | Black, counter | |

#### Script

> Most guys quote off feeling. That's why they're either broke or they lose every bid.
>
> Here's the whole sheet. Four numbers.
>
> **Access.** Can I get a truck to it, can I get a chipper to it, or am I carrying every piece a hundred feet to the road. Access is the number that surprises people and it can double a job.
>
> **What's underneath.** That's not a tree. That's a tree over a roof, a fence and a gas meter. What's underneath sets the risk, and the risk sets the price.
>
> **Man hours.** How many guys, how many hours. Then add thirty percent, because you're wrong.
>
> **Debris.** Where does the wood go. Chip it, haul it, or leave it stacked. That's a real cost and most guys give it away for free.
>
> Multiply it out, add your overhead, add your profit, and send it the same day. Same day is the part that wins the job.
>
> Comment QUOTE and I'll send you the sheet.

#### Edit notes
- Open on the sheet, not your face. The spreadsheet is the hook.
- Beat of silence after "add thirty percent, because you're wrong." That's the quotable line.
- Field name as text on each insert so people can follow it.

#### On-screen text
**"How to quote a tree job in 3 minutes"**

#### Caption
> Access. What's underneath it. Man hours plus 30%. Debris.
>
> Four numbers, three minutes, sent the same day. Same day is the part that wins the job, not the lowest number.
>
> This is the actual sheet I price with and it does the math for you. Comment QUOTE and I'll send it.

#### Pinned comment
> QUOTE = the sheet, free, no email. What's the one thing you always forget to price? 👇

**Before you post:** open the sheet link in a logged-out private browser window. If it doesn't load for a stranger, nothing else about this post matters.

## PART 3 · SHOOT THIS FOR THURSDAY

### The rain day talking head (shot 11)

Don't post it today, Slot B is taken. Shoot it while it's actually raining behind you and bank it for Thursday.

#### Script

> It's raining, so we're not on the tools. And rain days are not days off. They're the days that decide whether next month is any good.
>
> Here's what actually happens.
>
> **Every chain gets sharpened.** Not the ones that feel dull. All of them.
>
> **Every saw gets serviced.** Air filter, spark plug, bar flipped, sprocket checked.
>
> **Every rope and every saddle gets inspected.** And anything questionable gets retired. Not watched. Retired. A rope you're "keeping an eye on" is a rope you already don't trust.
>
> **Every outstanding quote gets a follow-up.** Every invoice goes out. Every unpaid one gets a phone call, not an email.
>
> And the crew gets paid anyway. Because a guy who loses a day's pay every time it rains starts looking for a job where he doesn't, and then you're training somebody new in March.
>
> Most tree companies lose money on rain days. The ones that don't are the ones that planned for them back in February.
>
> Comment QUOTE for the sheet I price with.

**Hook:** "It's raining. Here's what a tree company actually does."

That last point about paying the crew will start an argument in the comments. Good. Let it run and reply to every single one.
