# Production Bible · Beach Week Films

Every video from the Monday shoot: shots, script, edit timeline, on-screen text, caption, pinned comment.

**Universal rules for all of them**
- Vertical, 4K/30. Lock exposure by tapping and holding on your face or the sand before you roll.
- DJI mic for every talking head. Never the phone mic outdoors.
- Record 2x what you need. Every shot below lists record time and use time.
- Captions burned in, mid-lower third, away from the UI.
- Hook text top third, big, 3 to 8 words, white with a black stroke.
- End every one on black with **Day X / 90**.
- Post, then pin the comment within 30 seconds.

# FILM 1 · The Zen Garden
**Slot A, Monday · 28 seconds · The best thing you'll make this week**

## Shots

| # | Shot | Framing | Record | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Empty beach, you walk in | Locked off, wide, horizon on the lower third. You enter from the right. | 30 s | 3 s |
| 2 | The rake pulling sand | Phone almost on the ground, 6 inches from the teeth. Follow the pull. | 40 s | 4 s sped up 4x |
| 3 | Hands placing a stone | Close, from above, your hands filling the frame | 15 s | 2 s |
| 4 | You kneeling, tiny in frame | Phone 40 feet back, you in the bottom third, sky and water everywhere | 90 s | 5 s sped up 6x |
| 5 | The finished garden | Locked off, low, lines running away from camera toward the water | 20 s | 3 s |
| 6 | Nova's "oh noooo" + the foot | Already shot | — | 4 s |
| 7 | The wrecked garden | Locked off, same angle as shot 5 | 10 s | 3 s |

Shot 5 and shot 7 must be the **same framing**. That match cut is the whole joke.

## Script (voice-over, record in the truck after, quiet)

> I get up before everybody else because it's the only hour that's mine.
>
> Twenty minutes raking lines into wet sand. No reason. Nobody's going to see it. The tide takes it in two hours anyway.
>
> I do the same thing before I climb. Ten minutes of nothing, so the next three hours can be something.
>
> *(pause, let the finished garden sit)*
>
> *(Nova: "oh noooo")*
>
> Yeah. That's about right.

## Edit

| Time | Picture | Audio |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00 | Shot 1, you walk in. Hook text on. | Wind and water only |
| 0:03 | Shot 2, the rake, sped up | VO line 1 starts |
| 0:07 | Shot 4, you tiny, sped up | VO line 2 |
| 0:12 | Shot 3, the stone | VO continues |
| 0:14 | Shot 5, finished, held still | VO line 3 |
| 0:19 | Hold shot 5, nothing happens | Silence. Two full seconds. Do not cut early. |
| 0:21 | Nova's clip | "oh noooo" |
| 0:24 | The foot | Impact sound |
| 0:25 | Shot 7, wrecked, same frame | Silence |
| 0:27 | Black, "Day 8 / 90" | "Yeah. That's about right." |

**The two seconds of silence at 0:19 is the most important edit in the film.** Every instinct will tell you to cut it. Don't. That's where the laugh comes from.

No music. Wind and water are the soundtrack.

First frame: **"I spent 20 minutes making this."**

## Caption

>
> I take down trees that took 80 years to grow. She's got the right idea. Say "oh no" and do it anyway.

# FILM 3 · Five Systems
**Slot B, Monday · 48 seconds**

## Shot
One setup. Porch or deck, inlet behind you, you at eye level, phone at arm's length or propped. DJI mic on the collar. Sun behind the phone, not behind you.

Record it twice. Use the second take.

## Script

> Five systems. If you've got all five, you can leave for a week. If you're missing one, you're chained to the truck.
>
> **One.** Fifty percent deposit before a saw comes off the trailer. In writing, not a handshake. If they won't put money down, they were never going to pay you at the end either.
>
> **Two.** One guy who can run a day without you. Not a foreman on paper, a guy you'd hand the keys to. Took me three years to find him and I overpay him on purpose.
>
> **Three.** A one-page schedule. Not an app. One page, printed, on the dash. Everybody's looking at the same piece of paper.
>
> **Four.** A money meeting every Friday. Fifteen minutes. What came in, what's going out, what's owed. Do it weekly and you never get surprised.
>
> **Five.** A quote template that takes three minutes. If quoting takes an hour you'll stop quoting, and the guy who quotes fastest usually wins the job.
>
> That's the whole reason I'm sending emails off a deck instead of standing in somebody's yard.
>
> Comment QUOTE and I'll send you the template.

## Edit
- Open mid-sentence on "Five systems." Cut the breath before it.
- Put a **number card on screen** for each one: big "1", "2", "3", "4", "5" in the top corner as you say it. Costs nothing and doubles retention on list posts.
- Cut every pause between systems to zero. Hard cuts, no crossfades.
- Drop one B-roll insert of the water between system 3 and 4, 1 second, to break the talking head.
- End on "the template," black, "Day 8 / 90."

## On-screen text
**"5 systems that let me leave my company for a week"**

## Caption
> Deposit before the saw starts. A crew lead who can run a day without me. A one-page schedule. A money meeting every Friday. A quote template that takes 3 minutes.
>
> That's it. That's the whole reason I'm answering emails from a deck instead of a truck.
>
> Comment QUOTE and I'll send you the template.

# FILM 4 · How To Quote In 3 Minutes
**Slot B, Tuesday · 52 seconds · Your biggest keyword day**

## Shots
1. Talking head, same setup as Film 3, different shirt. 60 s record.
2. **Screen recording of your actual quote sheet**, scrolling slowly, numbers blurred. 40 s record, use 15 s in inserts.

## Script

> Most guys quote off feeling. That's why they're either broke or they lose every bid.
>
> Here's the whole sheet. Four numbers.
>
> **Access.** Can I get a truck to it. Can I get a chipper to it. Or am I carrying every piece a hundred feet to the road. Access is the number that surprises people and it can double a job.
>
> **What's underneath.** That's not a tree. That's a tree over a roof, a fence and a gas meter. What's underneath sets the risk, and the risk sets the price.
>
> **Man hours.** How many guys, how many hours. Then add thirty percent, because you're wrong.
>
> **Debris.** Where does the wood go. Chip it, haul it, or leave it stacked. That's a real cost and most guys give it away for free.
>
> Multiply it out, add your overhead, add your profit, and send it the same day. Same day is the part that wins the job.
>
> Comment QUOTE and I'll send you the sheet.

## Edit
- Open on the **screen recording**, not your face. The sheet is the hook.
- Cut to your face at 0:04 on "Most guys quote off feeling."
- Each of the four numbers gets a 2-second screen-recording insert of that line on the sheet, with the field name as text.
- "Add thirty percent because you're wrong" gets a beat of silence after it. That's the quotable line.
- End on your face, black, counter.

## On-screen text
**"How to quote a tree job in 3 minutes"**

## Caption
> Access. What's underneath it. Man hours plus 30%. Debris.
>
> Four numbers, three minutes, sent the same day. Same day is the part that wins the job.
>
> Comment QUOTE and I'll send you the exact sheet.

# FILM 5 · Why Tree Guys Stay Broke
**Slot B, Thursday · 46 seconds**

## Shot
One setup, different shirt, ideally standing rather than sitting. This one has some heat in it and standing reads that way.

## Script

> I know guys who are better climbers than me and make half what I make. It's never the climbing.
>
> It's three things.
>
> **One. No deposit.** They start work on a promise and then they're a bank. If you're floating somebody's job on your own money, you're not a tree company. You're a lender with a chainsaw.
>
> **Two. They quote on feeling.** Customer says "that seems like a lot" and they drop two grand on the spot. Your price isn't a negotiation, it's a calculation. If you can't explain it, you'll cave every time.
>
> **Three. They never follow up.** Quote goes out and that's the end of it. Half the jobs I've booked came from a text three days later that just said "still thinking about that oak?"
>
> None of that is skill. All of it is habit. And you can change a habit this week.
>
> Comment CLIMB for the Playbook.

## Edit
- Open mid-sentence on "better climbers than me."
- Number cards 1, 2, 3.
- **Hard beat of silence after "a lender with a chainsaw."** That's the screenshot line, give people a second to catch it.
- One B-roll insert of the water after point 2.
- End on "change a habit this week," black, counter.

# FILM 6 · The Mistake That Cost Me
**Slot B, Friday · 42 seconds · Fill in your real story**

## Shot
Sitting, close, quieter energy than Film 5. This is a confession, not a lecture.

## Script structure

> **The setup, one sentence.** "[Year]. [What kind of job]. [Something that made it feel routine.]"
>
> **What I did.** "[The specific wrong decision. Not 'I made a mistake', the actual decision.]"
>
> **What happened.** "[The consequence. Say the dollar number out loud.]"
>
> **What it actually cost.** "[Beyond the money. The client, the reputation, the week, the sleep.]"
>
> **The rule.** "So now I [the permanent rule you made]. Every time. No exceptions, even when it's a good customer."
>
> **The turn.** "I'm telling you this because nobody told me. That's a [dollar] lesson and you can have it for free."
>
> Comment CLIMB for the Playbook.

If you need a prompt, the four that most tree guys have: taking a job off a customer's description of access without walking it, not checking for a second power drop, no written change order when the scope grew, or starting without a deposit on somebody who seemed good for it.

## Edit
- Open on your face, mid-sentence, no hook text for the first half second. Then the text comes up. A confession that starts before the title feels more honest.
- No B-roll. Stay on your face the whole time. The discomfort is the content.
- The dollar number gets a text card on screen the moment you say it.
- End on "for free," black, counter.

## On-screen text
**"The mistake that cost me $[X]"**

## Caption
> [One line naming the mistake.]
>
> Cost me $[X] and about three weeks of sleep. Now I [the rule], every time, even on good customers.
>
> Nobody told me. You can have it for free.
>
> Comment CLIMB for the Playbook.

# FILM 7 · I'm Owed $30,000
**Slot B, Wednesday · 52 seconds · Likely your best B post of the week**

## Shot
Truck or deck, sitting, straight to camera. No B-roll, no cutaways. This one is you talking and nothing else.

## Script

> I'm owed thirty thousand dollars right now and it's been quiet for four days.
>
> No names. I'm not going to burn a client on the internet over a slow payment.
>
> But here's exactly what I do, because getting the work is the easy part. Getting paid is the job.
>
> **The invoice went out the day the work was done.** Not Friday. Not end of month. That day.
>
> **I've followed up twice, in writing.** Both times I asked two specific things: confirm you got it, and tell me the date it's getting paid. Not "just checking in." A question somebody actually has to answer.
>
> **This morning I called.** And when the person who signed the contract didn't pick up, I called the main line and asked for accounts payable directly. That's the move nobody makes. The person who hired you is not the person who cuts the check.
>
> **And I know exactly when my lien deadline is.** I'm nowhere near needing it. But the day you need to know that is too late to find out.
>
> I'll tell you how it goes.
>
> Comment CLIMB for the Playbook.

## Edit
- Open cold on "thirty thousand dollars." Hook text up immediately.
- Put **$30,000** on screen as a big number at 0:01 and leave it for two seconds.
- Each of the four moves gets a short text card: "Invoice same day." "Ask a real question." "Call AP, not the signer." "Know your lien deadline."
- "I'll tell you how it goes" is the last line before black. That's an open loop and it brings people back tomorrow.

## On-screen text
**"I'm owed $30,000 and it's been quiet for 4 days"**

## Caption
> Four days of silence on a $30,000 progress payment. No names, no company. Just what I actually do about it.
>
> Invoice the day the work is done. Follow up in writing with a question they have to answer. Call accounts payable, not the person who signed the contract. And know your lien deadline before you need it.
>
> Getting the work is the easy part. Getting paid is the job.
>
> I'll tell you how it goes. Comment CLIMB for the Playbook.
