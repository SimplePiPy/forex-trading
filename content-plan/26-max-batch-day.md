# The Max Batch Day

**Goal:** one day that produces two-plus weeks of content, so the rest of the week is pure tree service.

---

## The principle

**Shoot where the work is.** A home studio day gets you eight talking heads and nothing with reach. A jobsite day with the cameras running gets you eight talking heads *plus* a week of Climb footage, because the GoPro is recording whether you think about it or not.

If you're finishing the park job this week, tomorrow should be a jobsite day with a 45-minute talking-head block bolted onto the end. That is the single most productive content day available to you and it costs you almost no working time.

**The rule that protects the job:** the GoPro runs on its own, the ground camera gets three setups total, and every talking head happens at lunch or after the trucks are loaded. If content ever slows the crew down, content loses. A $40K job beats a Reel every time.

If tomorrow is not a jobsite day, skip to Part 4 and do the talking heads at home. You'll get 8 posts instead of 20.

## Part 2 · The jobsite day

### GoPro, helmet · zero effort, biggest return
Record from the ground up on every single climb. Stop it when your boots are back on dirt. Fresh battery each climb, card swap at lunch. Do not try to catch only the good parts.

After every tree, say the timestamp into your phone: *"Tree two, the top came off around forty minutes in, that's the one."* Ten seconds each time saves you an hour tonight.

### Ground camera · three setups, that's all
Hand the phone or the ZV-E10 to a groundie for five minutes, three times.

1. **Morning:** the look-up from the base, the gear on the tailgate, the crew arriving.
2. **Midday:** the drop, wide and static, held ten seconds after it lands. Plus the chipper feeding.
3. **End:** you walking to the truck, helmet coming off, low angle.

### The walkthrough · 8 minutes, first thing
Same format as before. Name each tree, point at the problem, say the plan in one sentence, say what could go wrong. Get a hero shot of each. This alone is two posts.

### Things worth rolling on if they happen
The moment something goes differently than planned. A customer conversation (ask first). Anybody on the crew doing something skilled. The check, the handshake, the invoice.

## Part 4 · The talking head block · 45 minutes, 8 scripts

End of day or lunch. One setup, change shirt every two or three, say each twice and keep the second take. Truck, tailgate, or standing in front of the work.

**Open mid-sentence every time.** Cut the breath, cut the setup, start on the first real word.

---

### 1 · The $30,000 update
**You owe this one.** On Day 11 you said on camera "I'll tell you how it goes."

**If it landed:**
> Remember the thirty thousand I was owed? It came in.
>
> Here's the one thing that moved it. I stopped emailing the person who signed the contract and I called the main line and asked for accounts payable by name. Different human, different desk, different incentive. The person who hires you is not the person who cuts the check, and most guys never figure that out.
>
> Invoice the day the work's done. Follow up in writing with a question they have to answer. Then call the department, not the person.
>
> Getting the work is the easy part.

**If it hasn't:**
> Still not paid. It's been [X] days on thirty thousand dollars.
>
> Here's where it stands and here's what I'm doing this week. [The specific next step.]
>
> I said I'd tell you either way. This is the either way. I'll keep telling you.

**Hook:** "Remember the $30,000?"

---

### 2 · The thing I check before every removal that has nothing to do with the tree
This is your best teaching post in weeks. Fill in the specifics in your own words, because the rules vary by state and you're the one standing behind it.

> Before I put a saw in anything, I walk the whole tree with my head up, looking for one thing. Nests.
>
> [Your version of: raptors are federally protected, you don't touch an active nest, and "I didn't see it" isn't a defense.]
>
> [What you do about squirrels and general birds versus what shuts a job down.]
>
> [When in the year it matters most in your area.]
>
> I tell the customer that on the estimate, not on the morning of. It costs me a job maybe [X] times a year. It has never cost me a fine, and I sleep fine.

**Hook:** "The thing I check before every removal that has nothing to do with the tree."

---

### 3 · How I'd get my first 10 customers with no money
> No ads. No website. Ten customers in two weeks. Here's exactly how.
>
> **One.** Drive the oldest neighborhood in your town and write down every address with a dead tree or a limb over a roof. Thirty addresses in an afternoon.
>
> **Two.** Handwritten note in the door. Not a flyer. "I'm a climber. Noticed the oak on the north side has deadwood over your driveway. Happy to look at it for free." Your name, your number, that's it.
>
> **Three.** Do the first three jobs at a fair price and photograph every single one. Before, during, after.
>
> **Four.** Ask every one of those customers for the name of one neighbor.
>
> That's the whole thing, it costs you gas and an afternoon, and it still works better than anything I pay for.

**Hook:** "How I'd get 10 customers this week with zero dollars."

### 4 · What this gear actually costs
Real numbers. Gear posts always perform and nobody in the trades posts honest ones.

> People ask what it costs to climb. Here's the real number for what's on me right now.
>
> Saddle: $[X]. Helmet: $[X]. Rope, and you need more than one: $[X]. Climbing saw: $[X]. Ground saw: $[X]. Spikes: $[X]. Rigging gear: $[X].
>
> That's $[total] before I own a truck, a chipper, or insurance.
>
> And here's the part that matters. [Your line about which piece you'd never cheap out on and why.]

**Hook:** "What's on me right now costs $[total]."

## Part 5 · The evening · 2 hours, then stop

1. **Dump everything** to the laptop and Drive. Both. Write the notes file with the timestamps you said out loud.
2. **Cut the top 4 first:** the best GoPro moment, the walkthrough, the $30,000 update, the nest post.
3. **Rough-cut 4 more.** Don't polish. Rough-cut beats unposted.
4. **Schedule everything you have** in Meta Business Suite. 7:00 AM and 12:30 PM.
5. **Save the pinned comments** to your notes app. Scheduling won't post them.
6. **Tick the bank list** so nothing gets suggested twice.

If you only finish six of the eight, schedule six and cut the rest Thursday night. A scheduled week beats a perfect one.
