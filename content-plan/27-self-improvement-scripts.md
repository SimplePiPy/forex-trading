# The Reach Library · Self-Improvement and Discipline Scripts

---

## The one rule that makes these work

This is the most saturated niche on the internet. A guy in a truck talking about discipline is competing with a hundred thousand bedroom philosophers, and he loses.

**You win exactly one way: every abstract claim anchors to a tree, a saw, a rope, a crew, or a job.**

Everybody else is speaking in metaphor. When you say discipline matters because the saw doesn't care how you feel, that isn't a metaphor. That's Tuesday. The second you drift into "most people don't understand" and "energy" and "showing up," you throw away the only thing you have that they don't.

Three more rules:

**Specificity beats profundity.** "I stopped drinking in March" beats "I let go of what wasn't serving me." Name the habit, the date, the number, the cost.

**Never open with "most people."** It's the most tired open in the niche. Talk about yourself and let people recognize themselves in it.

**Don't perform vulnerability on a schedule.** Shoot the easy ones in a batch. Shoot the hard ones, fear and habits especially, on a day you actually feel like talking about it. A rehearsed confession is obvious and it costs you more than skipping the post.

**Setups.** Sitting and close for the personal ones. Standing and wider for the instructional ones. Truck, tailgate, at the base of a tree, on the deck. Vary it so twelve posts don't look like one afternoon.

## HOLD THIS ONE

### Becoming a father
There is no version of this that isn't the announcement. Writing it carefully doesn't help, because "becoming a father" *is* the news.

It stays in the drawer until you and Mariah have decided. When you do, it's one of the best posts you'll ever make and it writes itself.

**The adjacent one you can post right now,** same emotional territory, zero disclosure:

> The men who taught me how to work never once told me they were proud of me.
>
> Not because they weren't. Because that wasn't the language.
>
> What they did instead was hand me the good saw. Let me make the cut they'd normally make. Stand at the bottom of the tree and not say anything while I figured it out.
>
> I spent years reading that as distance. It took me until about [age] to understand that was the whole sentence, and I just didn't speak the language yet.
>
> If somebody taught you to work, go tell them today. They probably never got told either.

**Hook:** "Nobody ever told me they were proud of me."

That'll be one of the highest-reach posts on this list and it involves nothing about your private life.

## 1 · Being a better partner

**Careful with this one.** Mariah is a person in it. The strong version is entirely about what *you* changed. No relationship dynamics, no what she needs, no what she said. Just your own behavior. That's more respectful and it's also better content, because "here's what I fixed" is useful and "here's how we work" is gossip.

> I used to come home from a twelve hour day and be useless.
>
> Physically in the room. Mentally still on the job, running tomorrow in my head while somebody was talking to me.
>
> And I told myself that was fine, because I was providing. That's the lie every man in the trades tells himself, and I believed it for years.
>
> Two things changed. I stopped taking calls after I pull into the driveway. The phone goes on the counter. If it's an actual emergency they'll call twice.
>
> And I stopped saying "I'm fine" when I wasn't. That's not stoic. It just makes somebody else do the guessing.
>
> I'm not good at this yet. I'm better than I was in March.

**Hook:** "I used to come home and be useless."

Ending on "I'm not good at this yet" is what keeps it from being a lecture.

## 2 · Leaving old habits behind

Only use what's true. If you've got a real one, name it plainly and give the date. If you don't, skip this rather than inventing one, because people can smell it.

**The structure:**

> [The habit. Say it plainly, no euphemism.]
>
> [When you stopped. A month and a year if you have it.]
>
> [The part nobody tells you: what got *harder* after, not easier.]
>
> [What actually filled the gap.]
>
> [The real reason, which is almost never discipline.]

**If it connects to the work, that's the version to use.** Something like:

> I quit [X] because of a tree.
>
> [The day. The height. What you noticed about your own hands or your own head.]
>
> Most people quit something for a reason they have to keep reminding themselves about. I quit because I went up in a state I had no business being in, and the tree didn't care, and I understood that the consequence wasn't going to be a rough morning. It was going to be a funeral.
>
> That's not discipline. That's arithmetic.

**Hook:** "I quit [X] because of a tree."

## 3 · Taking time off

> I took eight days off for the first time in years, and the entire time I was waiting for something to go wrong.
>
> Nothing did. The crew ran. The quotes went out. The phone got answered.
>
> And that bothered me more than if something had gone wrong.
>
> Because if nothing falls apart when I'm gone, then all those years I couldn't leave were never about the business needing me. They were about me needing to be needed.
>
> That's a harder thing to fix than a schedule.

**Hook:** "I took 8 days off and it bothered me that nothing broke."

This is the best one on the list. It's a real insight, almost every owner has lived it, and almost none of them have ever said it out loud.

## 4 · Resetting my nervous system

Keep it practical. No physiology claims, no supplements, no science you'd have to defend. Just what you do.

> I don't sit still in the morning because it's spiritual. I do it because I climb trees, and a shaking hand eighty feet up is a real problem.
>
> Four things. None of them cost money.
>
> **Ten minutes sitting still before the first climb.** Not clearing my head. Just letting it finish whatever it's already doing.
>
> **Cold water, first thing.** I hate it every single morning and I've never once regretted it.
>
> **Outside, early, no phone.** Twenty minutes before anything asks anything of me.
>
> **Sleep.** Which is the one everybody skips and the only one that isn't optional.
>
> That's the list. There's no supplement in it and there's nothing to buy.

**Hook:** "Four things, none of them cost money."

That last line is a quiet shot at the entire niche and people will feel it.

## 5 · Why discipline, and nothing else

> People think discipline is about wanting it more. It isn't.
>
> Discipline is building a life where the right thing is the easy thing.
>
> I don't get up at five because I'm tough. I get up at five because I go to bed at nine and the phone charges in the kitchen.
>
> I don't skip the ten minutes before a climb because my gear goes in the same spot every night, so the routine starts itself before I've decided anything.
>
> Motivation is a feeling. You cannot rely on a feeling eighty feet up with a running saw. So I stopped relying on it and started building around it.
>
> That's the whole thing. Set it up so you don't have to decide.

**Hook:** "Discipline isn't wanting it more."

## 6 · Fear

**Your single strongest post in this niche, and nobody else can make it.**

> I'm scared at work. Regularly.
>
> Not nervous. Scared. There's a moment on a bad tree where my whole body says don't, and it took me years to learn that moment is information, not weakness.
>
> Here's what I do with it now. I stop. I come down if I need to. And I say out loud to the crew exactly what I'm worried about, because fear you say out loud gets smaller and fear you hide gets bigger.
>
> Every guy I know who got hurt badly got hurt on a day he pushed through that feeling to prove something to somebody.
>
> Courage isn't ignoring it. Courage is hearing it, all the way, and then making a decision anyway.

**Hook:** "I'm scared at work. Regularly."

## 7 · An hour alone with your own head

> Almost nobody gets this anymore and I get it every day.
>
> An hour, eighty feet up, no phone, nothing to look at, nothing to scroll. Just me and whatever my brain decides to bring up.
>
> The first year, that was the worst part of the job. Now it's most of the reason I do it.
>
> You're not bored when you put the phone down. You're just not used to being alone with yourself, and the only way through that is through it.
>
> Try an hour this week with nothing. You'll hate it. Do it again Thursday.

**Hook:** "An hour, 80 feet up, no phone."

## 8 · What this work costs my body

Nobody in the trades posts this honestly and it will travel.

> I'm [age]. Here's the bill this job has handed me so far. [Be specific and real. Shoulders, back, hands, hearing, knees, whatever it actually is.]
>
> Nobody in the trades talks about this and they should, because it's the part that decides whether you're still doing this at fifty.
>
> [What you do now that you didn't at twenty-two. Stretching, gym, the way you lift, the gear you finally bought.]
>
> The men I looked up to at twenty are all broken at fifty. I'd rather be the one who stretched and got made fun of for it.

**Hook:** "What this job has cost my body."

## 9 · Enough

> How much money is enough. Actual answer.
>
> [Your real number, or your real framing.]
>
> For a long time the answer was "more," which isn't an answer, it's a treadmill with a nicer view.
>
> Here's where I landed. I'm not chasing a number. I'm chasing a Tuesday where I don't have to take a job I don't want to take.
>
> That's it. That's the whole finish line.

**Hook:** "How much is enough. Real answer."

## 10 · The phone problem

Self-aware in a way this niche almost never is, and it's about something you're actively living.

> I'm building an audience and trying to be present at the same time, and those two things fight every single day.
>
> Here's my rule, and I break it. The phone opens once a day for twenty minutes. Replies, DMs, comments. Then it closes.
>
> Because if I'm going to sit here and tell you to build a life you actually get to live, I can't spend that life behind a screen telling you about it.
>
> I'm not winning this one yet. I'm just not pretending it isn't happening.

**Hook:** "I'm building an audience and trying to be present. Those fight."

---

## 11 · The first hour decides the day

> The first hour of my day has nothing in it that anybody else chose.
>
> No phone, no email, no radio, nobody's voice but mine. Coffee, ten minutes sitting, one page written by hand.
>
> Then I let the world in.
>
> Here's why. Whatever gets to you first sets the tone, and if you let your phone go first, you spend the whole day reacting to other people's decisions.
>
> One hour. That's all I'm protecting. Everything after that belongs to the job.

**Hook:** "The first hour of my day belongs to nobody else."

---

## 12 · The thing you're avoiding is the job

> On a tree job there's always one cut you don't want to make. The awkward one, over the house, at the wrong angle.
>
> Amateurs save it for last. Every time.
>
> You do it first, while the crew is fresh and you're sharp and there's daylight left to fix a mistake. Save it for four o'clock and you make it tired with a tired crew and no margin.
>
> Whatever you've been putting off for three weeks, that's the cut. And you're going to end up making it at four o'clock.

**Hook:** "Amateurs save the hard cut for last."

That's a genuinely great post because the metaphor is your actual job, not borrowed.

## How to deploy these

**Don't post them back to back.** Two a week maximum, in the Slot B position, mixed in with the tree and business content. A feed that's suddenly all mindset reads like a pivot and your existing audience came for trees.

**Rank them by what to post first:**
1. Taking time off
2. Fear
3. The hard cut first
4. The men who taught me
5. An hour alone
6. Being a better partner
7. Discipline
8. Nervous system
9. The phone problem
10. What it costs my body
11. Enough
12. Old habits, when you're ready

**Pinned comments** should be a real question, not a CTA, on all of these. "What's the cut you've been putting off? 👇" gets ten times the replies of "comment CLIMB."

**Put CLIMB on maybe a third of them.** Emotional posts with a funnel attached feel like bait. Let most of these just be posts.
