## 1 · The payment (do this first, before 10 AM)

You leave Saturday. If this isn't locked today, it sits untouched for eight days and you're chasing it on September 27 instead of banking it on September 18.

### Make the call, don't send the email

A mobile home park is almost always run by a property management company, which means an office, an accounting cycle, and a person whose whole job is to slow payments down politely. Email goes in a queue. A phone call gets a decision.

Call the person who signed the contract, not accounting. Ask them to walk it over.

**The script:**

> "Hey [name], quick one. We're on schedule to finish this week. The progress payment on the contract comes due Wednesday, and I'm out of town starting Saturday. I want to make sure it's in motion before I go so nothing sits. Two questions. Who do I send the invoice to so it gets processed this week, not next? And can you do ACH instead of a check? I'll send you the routing and account today."

Then the one that actually matters:

> "What's the soonest you've ever turned one of these around? I'm not asking you to break your process, I'm asking what's actually possible."

Write down the name of the person in accounting and their direct line. Get it in writing today, even if it's just a reply-all confirming the date.

### Method, ranked by how fast you see money

| Method | Speed | Cost to you | When to use it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ACH / direct deposit** | 1–3 business days | Free or a few dollars | Default ask. Every property manager can do it. |
| **Wire** | Same day if before their cutoff | Usually $15–$30, sometimes on them | When it's large and you need it before Saturday |
| **Check, picked up in person** | Same day you pick it up | Free | If they insist on a check. Never let them mail it before a trip. |
| **Check, mailed** | 5–10 days, plus it can get lost | Free | Avoid this week entirely |
| **Card via Square or Stripe** | 1–2 days | ~2.9% plus 30¢ | Last resort. On a $20,000 payment that's roughly $580 you're handing over. |

Ask for ACH. Offer to pick up a check the same day as a fallback. Only mention a card if they're about to walk.

### Before you hang up
- [ ] Invoice sent today, not Wednesday. Dated today, terms stated, the contract's payment milestone quoted on it.
- [ ] Routing and account number sent by whatever method their office prefers. Confirm they received it.
- [ ] A name and direct line in accounting written down.
- [ ] A date in writing.
- [ ] Ask now what completion paperwork they need Friday, so the final payment isn't held up by a form you've never seen.

### One caution on filming this
Film yourself talking about chasing a payment. Do not film the client, name the park, show the invoice with their information on it, or say anything that makes them look bad. You are still owed money by these people and the internet is forever. Blur or crop every number and name.

## 3 · The week plan (2:00 PM, on paper)

Write these numbers down before you decide anything:

1. Trees down and cleaned up as of Monday night: ___
2. Trees left: ___
3. Of those, how many are the hard ones (over a home, over power, crane or bucket needed): ___
4. Crew available Wed/Thu/Fri: ___ guys
5. Realistic trees per day at full crew: ___
6. Dump and chip runs still needed: ___

Then: **trees left ÷ trees per day = days needed.** If that's more than three, you have four honest options. Pick one today, not Thursday at 4 PM.

**Option A · Longer days.** 6 AM to dark, Wed through Fri. Buy the crew lunch and pay the overtime. Works if you're one day short, not two. Costs you the most and it's the one most likely to put somebody in the hospital on Friday afternoon when everyone's tired. If you pick this, the hard trees go first, Wednesday morning, fresh.

**Option B · The crew works a day without you.** If you've got a lead who can run the ground work, cleanup, chipping and hauling, that's a full day of production that doesn't need a climber. Stack all the climbing into the days you're there.

**Option C · Rent the problem away.** A bucket truck or a crane for one day turns three days of technical removals into one. It's $800 to $2,500 depending on what and where, and on a $40,000 job that math usually works. Call this morning, not Wednesday, because you won't get one same-day.

**Option D · Split the job. This is probably the answer.**

Finish everything that's a hazard or that the park needs gone before you leave. Schedule the rest for the week of the 28th. Then call the park manager today, before they hear it from a crew member:

> "Here's where we're at. We'll have every tree that's over a home or over power down and cleaned up by Friday. There's [N] left in the back that aren't a hazard to anybody. I'm out the week of the 19th, and I'd rather do those right on the 28th than rush them Friday afternoon with a tired crew. Does that work for you? And I'd like to bill the completed portion Friday so we're square before I go."

That last sentence is the whole reason to make this call today. You are converting a "completion payment" into a second progress payment, which means you get paid Friday instead of October.

Most clients say yes to this, because "I'd rather do it right than rush it with a tired crew" is the sentence every property manager wants to hear and almost never does. The ones who say no tell you something useful about them.

**Whatever you pick, write down:**
- Wednesday: which trees, who's there, what gets hauled
- Thursday: same
- Friday: same, plus the walkthrough, the photos, the invoice, and the pickup
- What you tell the crew tomorrow morning at 6:30, in one sentence

## 7 · What this does to the calendar

**"Day 5. Every tree over a house is down. Here's what I'm leaving for the 28th, and why."**

Which is honestly a better post, because finishing is a flex and *choosing not to rush* is a lesson. The completion post moves to the week of the 28th and becomes a Day 15-ish payoff: "Remember the job I didn't finish before vacation?"

Everything else in the calendar holds. The beach week Slot A bank now comes from Monday, Wednesday, Thursday and Friday footage instead of five days. That's still plenty. Shoot everything Wednesday through Friday like the cameras are the point, because from here on out, they are.
