# Day 9 · Fog and Rain · Shoot This Before It Burns Off

Fog is the most expensive-looking thing you will ever get for free. It separates everything into layers, turns any person into a silhouette, and makes a phone look like a cinema camera. You have a window of maybe two hours. Go now, read the rest on the sand.

---

## 1 · Settings, before you walk out

**Underexpose by about a third.** Your phone sees a bright gray field and tries to make it middle gray, which blows the fog out and makes it look like a white screen. Tap to focus on your subject, then **drag the little sun icon down** until the fog looks slightly darker than your eye sees it. Then press and hold to lock AE/AF so it doesn't hunt.

**Use the 2x or 3x lens, not the ultrawide.** Fog looks best compressed. A longer lens stacks the layers on top of each other and that stacking is the whole effect. Ultrawide spreads the fog thin and it just looks hazy.

**Put something dark in every frame.** Fog with nothing in it is a blank screen. A person, a piling, a rail, a dark line of wet sand. The dark object is what makes the fog read as fog.

**Carry a microfiber cloth and wipe the lens before every single shot.** This is the whole ballgame in rain. One drop you didn't notice ruins a take you can't reshoot. Check the lens, wipe, then roll.

**Furry windscreen on the DJI mic** or don't bother with voice outdoors today. Wind plus fog plus an open mic is unusable.

**Shoot 4K/30, not 60.** Fog has almost no detail to resolve and the lower frame rate holds a touch more light.

Rain on the lens is a look, but only on purpose, and only on one shot. Never on a talking head.

---

## 2 · The shot list

Ordered by how badly you'd regret missing it.

| # | Shot | How | Why it works |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **You walking away into the fog** | Phone locked off on a rail or in the sand, you walk directly away until you're a smudge. Let it run 45 seconds. Don't look back. | The single best shot available today. It's the whole Stillness pillar in one frame. |
| 2 | **The dredger emerging** | 3x lens, locked off, let it come out of the murk | An industrial machine appearing out of nothing. If it's running, this is your Slot A. |
| 3 | **Pilings or a pier receding** | 3x, each one lighter than the last | Free depth. This is the shot fog exists for. |
| 4 | **No horizon** | Point at where water meets sky and find the spot where there isn't one | Genuinely disorienting and people stop on it |
| 5 | **Rain hitting the water** | As close as you can safely get, 3x, locked off, 30 seconds | Sound-on ASMR. Huge watch time on a loop. |
| 6 | **Rain on the sand / the zen garden** | Overhead, close, the drops pocking the surface | If the garden survived, rain slowly erasing it is a better ending than the foot |
| 7 | **You on the deck under cover, watching it** | Phone inside looking out through the glass, you small, rain on the window between camera and you | The warmest shot you'll get all week |
| 8 | **Coffee, window, rain** | Close, shallow, the cup in focus and the rain soft behind | Slot C filler that always works |
| 9 | **Your hands, wet** | Close. Rain on skin reads as work even when it isn't | Cutaway |

**Hold every locked-off shot at least 20 seconds.** In fog, nothing happens fast, and the slow reveal is the point. You will want to cut early. Don't.

## 3 · The angle that ties it back to the trees

This is the one that makes today more than pretty footage.

**Fog on the water looks exactly like fog in a canopy at dawn.** You know that and almost nobody watching does.

So the Slot A or Slot C today is: *"This is what the top of a tree looks like at six in the morning in October."* Shoot the fog, say that line, and suddenly your beach week reconnects to the thing people follow you for. It has been eight days since you showed them anything about climbing, and this brings the Climb pillar back without a tree in frame.

## 4 · Today's three posts

### Slot A · The dredger in fog, or the crane footage from the bank
If the dredger is running, shoot it and post it today, weather beats schedule. If it isn't, run the crane clip from the bank as planned.

**Hook:** "A machine the size of a building, appearing out of nothing."

> Fog so thick this morning you couldn't see fifty feet. Then this came out of it.
>
> It's pulling sand off the bottom of the inlet and pumping it back onto the beach a mile at a time. Rebuilding a coastline.
>
> Day 9 of 90.

### Slot B · The rain day post
This is today's best business idea and it's real, because it is actually raining.

**Hook:** "It's raining. Here's what a tree company does on a rain day."

> Rain days are not days off. They're the days that decide whether next month is any good.
>
> Chains get sharpened. Every saw gets serviced. Ropes and saddles get inspected, and anything questionable gets retired, not "watched."
>
> Every outstanding quote gets followed up. Every invoice goes out. Every unpaid one gets a phone call.
>
> And the crew gets paid anyway, because a guy who loses a day's pay every time it rains starts looking for a job where he doesn't.
>
> Most tree companies lose money on rain days. The ones that don't are the ones that planned for them in February.
>
> Comment QUOTE for the sheet I price with.

That fourth point is the one people will argue about in the comments, which is exactly what you want.

### Slot C · The fog walk
The walking-away shot. This is the post.

**Edit:** open on the locked-off frame with you already small. Hold 5 seconds of silence before any voice. Let the fog swallow you. End on the empty frame, hold 2 seconds, then black.

**Hook:** "This is what the top of a tree looks like at 6 AM in October."

> Fog on the water this morning. Same thing you see at eighty feet on a cold fall morning, when the ground disappears and it's just you and the part of the tree you can reach.
>
> That's the hour I actually do this for.
>
> Day 9 of 90. Comment STILL for the 10 minutes I do before every climb.

No music. Rain and water only.

## 5 · Stories

1. Out the window, first thing: "Fogged in. This is the best thing that could have happened today."
2. Poll: "Fog or sun for filming?" Most people say sun. They're wrong and you'll tell them why.
3. A raw 10-second clip from the beach, no edit.
4. Text over the fog: "Free production value. Costs nothing. Most people stay inside."
5. The rain-day list as a text card: sharpen, service, inspect, follow up, invoice, pay the crew.
6. Evening: the number, one honest line, "tomorrow: [X]."

---

## 6 · Keep it dry

One real thing. Your phone is the entire production right now and you have a job to finish next week that depends on being reachable.

Ziplock bag with a hole for the lens if it gets heavy. Dry the charging port before you plug in, wet ports are how phones die. And don't set it on the rail in wind.
