# Day 3 · Back On The Jobsite · Three Trees

**Gear:** GoPro on the helmet, iPhone in the chest pocket. That's it. That's enough.

**The story of the day writes itself:** you're behind, you leave Saturday, and three trees have to come down today. Morning walkthrough sets the stakes. End of day pays them off. That's a complete arc in one day and it's the most watchable structure there is.

**One note on the counter:** whatever number you put on screen has to match the real day count from Day 1. If this posts Thursday it's Day 4. Never let the math break, people check.

---

## 1 · The walkthrough (film this first, it's the whole day)

Most walkthrough videos are unusable because they're a guy wandering around pointing a phone at nothing. Here's how to make one that's a Reel, a YouTube segment, and the setup for the payoff.

**Shoot it on the iPhone, handheld, walking.** Vertical. Talk the whole time like you're explaining it to a new guy on the crew.

**Structure it as a countdown of three.** Walk to each tree in the order you're taking them. At each one, do four things, about 20 seconds each:

1. **Name it.** "Tree one. Red oak, about eighty feet."
2. **Point at the problem.** The roof it's over, the line it's near, the lean, the deadwood, the neighbor's fence. Physically point. Get it in frame.
3. **Say the plan in one sentence.** "This one comes down in pieces off a rigging line because there's nowhere to drop it."
4. **Say what could go wrong.** This is the part nobody films and it's the part that makes it watchable. "If the top goes left instead of forward it's on that carport."

Then one closing line to camera before you gear up:

> "Three trees. We're a day behind and I leave for the beach Saturday. Let's see how this goes."

**Get a hero shot of each tree** while you're there. Stand back, hold the phone still, frame the whole tree with something for scale. That's your before shot for each one and you'll want all three tonight.

**Total time: eight minutes.** Do it before the crew fires anything up, while it's quiet and the light is low.

## 2 · The three posts

### Slot A · The walkthrough (post 7:00–8:00 AM, 45–60 s)

Cut the walkthrough down hard. Keep the pointing, cut the walking.

**First-frame text:** "Three trees. One day. I leave Saturday."

Structure:
- 0:00 you already walking and talking, pointing at tree one
- 0:12 hero shot of tree one, text: "1 of 3"
- 0:15 tree two, the problem, the risk
- 0:27 hero shot, text: "2 of 3"
- 0:30 tree three, the hard one
- 0:42 hero shot, text: "3 of 3"
- 0:48 closing line to camera
- Black, **"Day [X] / 90"**

**Caption:**
> Three trees have to come down today. We're a day behind and I'm on the road Saturday.
>
> Tree 1: [species], [what makes it hard].
> Tree 2: [species], [what makes it hard].
> Tree 3: the one I'm actually thinking about.
>
> Every job starts with this walk. Ten minutes of looking is worth two hours of cutting.
>
> Comment CLIMB for the Playbook I'm running.

**Pinned:** CLIMB = the Playbook. Which one would you take first? 👇

That pinned question is doing work. Arborists in your comments will argue about the order and every argument is reach.

### Slot B · How a pro reads a job (post 12:30, 40–50 s)

Same walkthrough footage, completely different post. This is the Operator one and it's the one that gets saved.

**First-frame text:** "What I see in a tree that you don't."

Pick the single hardest tree from the morning and break down the read:

> Everybody looks at a tree and sees a tree. Here's what I'm actually looking at.
>
> First, where does it want to go? Every tree has a lean and a heavy side and it's going there whether I like it or not. *(point at it in the footage)*
>
> Second, what's underneath it? That's not a tree, that's a carport, a fence, and a gas meter. That's what sets the price, not the tree.
>
> Third, what's it going to do when I cut it? [The specific thing: the hinge, the barber chair risk, the dead top, the co-dominant stem.]
>
> Fourth, how do I get the wood out of here? Half the cost of a job is the part after the tree's already on the ground.
>
> That's a ten-minute walk and it's the difference between a four-hour job and somebody's insurance claim.
>
> Comment QUOTE for the sheet I price these with.

**Caption:**
> Where it wants to go. What's underneath it. What it does when I cut it. How the wood gets out.
>
> Ten minutes of looking. That's the whole job.
>
> Comment QUOTE for my pricing sheet.

**Pinned:** QUOTE = the pricing sheet. What's the one thing you always check first? 👇

### Slot C · The payoff (post 7:00 PM, 30–40 s)

This is the post the morning was built for. Do not skip it, even if you're wrecked.

**First-frame text:** "This morning I said three trees. Here's how it went."

- 0:00 two seconds of the morning hero shot of tree one, then cut to it on the ground. Same frame, before and after.
- Same for tree two.
- Same for tree three.
- Then you, in the truck at the end of the day, helmet off, 15 seconds, honest:

> Three trees. [What actually happened, including whatever went sideways.] We're back on schedule / we're still a day behind and here's what I'm doing about it. Day [X].

If you only got two down, **say you only got two down.** That post is better than the one where you got three. Nobody believes the guy who always wins.

**Caption:**
> This morning: three trees standing. Tonight: [what's true].
>
> [One line on what went right or wrong.]
>
> Day [X] of 90.

**Pinned:** CLIMB = the Playbook. 👇

## 3 · GoPro discipline (you're moving fast today, so keep it simple)

Four rules, that's all:

1. **Hit record at the base, before you leave the ground. Stop it when your feet are back on dirt.** Don't try to start and stop for the good parts. You'll miss them.
2. **Fresh battery every climb.** 4K/60 gives you 60 to 80 minutes. Three trees is three batteries minimum.
3. **Swap the card at lunch** whether it needs it or not.
4. **After each tree, say the timestamp out loud into your phone.** "Tree two, the top came off around forty minutes in, that's the one." Ten seconds now saves you an hour tonight.

**The three moments that matter most today,** in order: the top coming off the hard tree, the biggest piece landing, and anything that surprises you. If the GoPro only caught those three things it was a good day.

## 4 · iPhone moments (five, that's it)

You're behind schedule. Don't turn yourself into a film crew.

1. **6:30** The walkthrough. Eight minutes.
2. **After tree one is down** 10 seconds, phone, the stump and the pile: "One."
3. **Lunch** Hand the phone to a groundie and get 20 seconds of you working, from the ground, looking up. You have almost no footage of yourself from the outside.
4. **After tree three** 10 seconds: "Three." Or "Two, and here's why."
5. **Truck at the end** the Slot C talking head.

## 5 · Stories (9, all iPhone, counter in the corner on every one)

1. **6:15** Face, dark, truck: "Back on the jobsite. Three trees today or I'm in trouble Saturday."
2. **6:45** 15 seconds of the walkthrough, raw: "Here's what we're up against."
3. **7:00** Poll: "Which one first? The one over the carport / The one on the line"
4. **Mid-morning** Reshare Slot A: "Walkthrough's up."
5. **After tree one** Photo of it down: "1 of 3. ✅"
6. **Lunch** 10 seconds of the crew eating or the chipper running. Text: "Halfway. Legs are gone."
7. **After tree two** "2 of 3. ✅"
8. **End of day** "3 of 3 ✅" or the honest version. Face, 15 seconds.
9. **8:30** "Day [X]: [X] followers. Tomorrow: [what's left]."

That 1-of-3, 2-of-3, 3-of-3 run is the most underrated thing in this whole plan. It costs nothing, it's a real countdown with a real outcome, and people will tap through your Stories all day to see if you make it.

**Broadcast Channel:** morning "Three trees today. Behind schedule. Walkthrough's coming at 7." Evening: the count and the number.
