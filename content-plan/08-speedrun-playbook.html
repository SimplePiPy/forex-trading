Part 1 · The Method

The one idea

Your trade is the most watchable thing you own. A roofer on a ridge, a welder's arc, a tree coming down 60 feet over a house. People who sit at desks cannot look away. You've been walking past a million views a day because it's "just work" to you.

The internet doesn't want you to become a content creator. It wants to watch you do your job, and then it wants to know you.

The four kinds of posts

Every post I make is one of these four. Never two at once.

Pillar What it is Job it does Share of posts
The Work Point-of-view footage of the actual job. The dangerous part, the skilled part, the satisfying part. Reach. Strangers find you here. 40%
The Numbers "Day X." What happened, what worked, what didn't. The graph. Authority. This is why people want to learn from you instead of just watch you. 25%
The Business How the money works. Quoting, crews, deposits, collecting, mistakes. Buyers. The people who'll pay you watch this. 20%
The Quiet Who you are when the saw's off. Morning routine, family, what you read, why you do it. Retention. Why people stay. 15%

For your trade, swap the specifics. A concrete guy's "Work" is the pour and the finish. A roofer's is the tear-off and the ridge. A mechanic's is the diagnosis and the moment it starts. Same four pillars.

The three-slot day

Three posts a day, same times every day. This is the schedule I'm running, including from the beach.

Time Slot Pillar Length
7:00 AM A The Work 12–45 seconds
12:30 PM B The Numbers or The Business 30–50 seconds
7:00 PM C The Quiet, or an experiment 15–25 seconds

Every post goes to Instagram first, then gets uploaded natively (no watermark, never a shared link) to TikTok and YouTube Shorts. Ten to fifteen Stories a day. Reply to every comment in the first hour.

If three a day is too much right now, do one a day at 7 AM and never miss. One a day beats three a day for two weeks and then nothing.

The ten rules

  1. The first frame is the whole game. If someone scrolls in the first two seconds, nothing else matters. Open on the most extreme visual you have or the hook text on a clean frame. Never your logo. Never you saying "hey guys."
  2. One idea per post. If it takes two sentences to explain, it's two posts.
  3. Post for strangers, not for your customers. Assume nobody knows you. Never say "as you guys know."
  4. Watch time and shares beat likes. Cut so the video loops. Give people a reason to send it: "send this to the guy on your crew who…"
  5. Native everything. Shot vertical. Captions burned in. No watermarks. No reposts.
  6. Reply to every comment in the first hour. Then again that night. The first hour is when the platform decides how far to push it.
  7. Post the same winner three ways. A sequel, a re-hook with new text on the same footage, and a carousel of the lesson. 97% of people never saw the first one.
  8. Turn on Trial Reels. It's a setting on Instagram that shows a post to non-followers only. One post a day goes out as a Trial. If it hits, you promote it.
  9. Never break the streak. Post on the day the job goes bad. Post from the hospital. The day you almost quit is the post that does five million.
  10. Sell with proof, not pressure. The graph and the day counter do the selling. You just ask for the DM.

Part 4 · The Setup (you need less than you think)

You do not need a camera. You need your phone, one mount, and one microphone.

Need Cheapest thing that works What I'm using
Point-of-view footage A phone chest mount ($15) or a used GoPro ($150) on a helmet mount GoPro Hero 11 on the helmet, 4K/60, horizon lock on
Talking to camera Your phone, arm's length, eye level, sun behind the camera iPhone 15 Pro Max
Sound A $25 wired lav mic clipped to your collar. Never the phone mic outdoors. DJI wireless mic
Steady ground shots Lean on the truck. A $30 mini tripod. Sony ZV-E10 on a gimbal, run by whoever's on the ground
Editing CapCut, free, on your phone CapCut desktop, then an editor once volume is up

Settings that matter: film vertical for anything short. Turn on stabilization. Record the whole job, not just the highlights, because you don't know which 10 seconds will be the one. Carry a battery bank.

The ground shot list (tape it to the truck, hand your phone to anyone):

  1. The look-up: camera at the base, tilt up to the work (5 seconds)
  2. The big moment: wide, static, hold 10 seconds
  3. The crew working: slow walk toward them (10 seconds)
  4. The tool doing its job: close-up (5 seconds)
  5. You walking to the truck at the end, helmet off, low angle (10 seconds)
  6. The check, the handshake, or the tailgate (10 seconds)

Part 5 · The Profile That Turns a Spike Into Followers

When a post hits, thousands of people tap your name in the same hour. Your profile has to be ready before that happens, not after.

Bio template:

[Name] · [Trade] · [City]
Day X/90 of [your goal] 🌲
[One line about who you help]
DM [KEYWORD] for [the free thing] 👇
[link]

The keyword. Pick one word. Put it in every caption, every pinned comment, every Story. Set up Instagram's comment-to-DM automation (free, in the app) or ManyChat so anyone who comments the word gets a link automatically. Make the automated message one line, one link, one question: "Here it is. What's your trade?"

The pinned comment. Posted within 30 seconds of every Reel: the keyword and a question. Comments with replies get pushed further.

The grid. Pick a cover frame for every Reel with your face or the height or the mess. Put the hook text on it. Someone who lands on your profile from a viral post decides in three seconds whether to follow.

Highlights. "Day 1", "The Work", "Numbers", "The Crew". Save every Story sequence.

Part 6 · The Sunday Review (60 minutes, every week)

This is where the growth actually comes from. Skip it and you're just posting.

  1. Pull the numbers. Top 3 posts and bottom 3 posts, ranked by follows per view, not views. Instagram shows "follows" in each Reel's insights.
  2. Why did the top 3 work? Write one sentence each. Hook type, pillar, length, first frame.
  3. Why did the bottom 3 flop? One sentence each. Usually it's one of: opened on you talking, two ideas, no payoff.
  4. Schedule a sequel, a re-hook, and a carousel for each of the top 3 this week.
  5. Kill any format that's flopped three weeks straight.
  6. Write next week's 21 hooks in one sitting.
  7. Film all seven talking-head posts in one 60-minute block. Change shirts.
  8. Check: keyword automation firing? Link in bio right? Day counter updated?
