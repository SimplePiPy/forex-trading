## 1 · Before Ant walks in: understand what this actually is

Ant is **Founding Sprint member zero.** You are about to deliver, for free, the thing you're going to charge $1,500 for on Day 21. That's the right move, but only if you treat it like the real thing from minute one.

What that means practically:

- **Screenshot his numbers today, before you touch anything.** Follower count, last 9 posts with views, his bio as it currently reads. Put it in a folder called `Ant_Day0`. In 30 days that screenshot is worth more to your business than anything you'll post this week.
- **Ask him straight, at the start:** "Can I document this? Your numbers, your posts, the before and after. You're the first guy I'm doing this with and I want to show people it works." He'll say yes. Ask anyway, on camera if he's comfortable.
- **Ask for three things instead of money.** A short video testimonial at 30 days if it works. Two introductions to other tree company owners. And his honest feedback on what was confusing, because that's how you find the holes before somebody pays you.

Write those three things down in front of him. That's what makes it a program instead of a favor, and it's the same conversation you'll have with paying clients later.

**The other thing to protect:** do not build Ant a copy of your account. Two arborists in the same region posting the same thing is two guys splitting one audience. His brand has to be genuinely his, and the two accounts should send people to each other. §3 handles that.

## 2 · The session agenda (90 minutes, in this order)

Don't freestyle it. Run the agenda. This is the rehearsal for every paid call you'll ever do.

Get numbers on paper, not vibes.

- Crew size, equipment, what jobs he *can* take that he isn't getting

### Minute 10–25 · Positioning
Land on one sentence. Don't leave this section until he can say it without reading it. Draft in §3.

### Minute 25–40 · The four pillars, his version
Fill in the table in §3 together. He should leave knowing which four buckets every post falls into.

### Minute 40–55 · Profile surgery, done live on his phone
Bio, name field, keyword, highlights, covers. Don't send him home to do it. Do it while he sits there. This is the part that converts a viral post into followers and it takes 15 minutes.

### Minute 55–75 · His first 7 days
Write all seven hooks with him. Not concepts, actual on-screen text. §3 has the draft.

### Minute 75–90 · The rules and the commitment

- Reply to every comment in the first hour.
- Send you his numbers every Sunday night.
- Next check-in: a call on the 28th when you're back from the beach.

## 3 · Appalachian Arborist · the starter plan

### The positioning

The name is the asset. "Appalachian Arborist" isn't a tree service name, it's a *place* name, and place is the strongest brand material on the internet right now. Nobody scrolls past Appalachia.

So he is not "a tree guy who also lives in the mountains." He is **the arborist of these mountains.**

Draft sentence for him to sharpen:

> "I'm an arborist in the Appalachians. I take down the trees that can't be taken down anywhere else, on ground nobody else will work, and I'll teach you everything I know about the trees that grow here."

Three words for his profile: **Mountain. Hardwood. Steep.**

**Why this doesn't compete with you:** your account is a climber running a speedrun and teaching tradesmen how to build a brand. His is a regional authority on Appalachian trees and the work they demand. Different promise, different audience, overlapping viewers. You cross-post each other and both grow. Say that to him out loud so he never feels like he's copying you.

### His four pillars

| Pillar | What it is | Job | Share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Mountain** | Steep ground, brutal access, the roped-in stuff flat-country guys never see. Trucks that can't get there. Trees on a 40-degree slope. | Reach. Nobody has this footage. | 40% |
| **The Species** | Oak, poplar, hemlock, ash. What's killing them. Hemlock woolly adelgid. Emerald ash borer. What a homeowner should look for. | Authority. This is the differentiator. | 25% |
| **The Storm** | Ice storms, wind, the 6 AM call, trees on houses, mountain roads. | Reach plus local customers. | 20% |
| **The Craft** | The rigging, the gear, the crew, the cost, what he charges and why. | Buyers. | 15% |

**The Species pillar is the whole thing.** Almost no arborist posts it and it is genuinely fascinating. A hemlock stand dying from an insect the size of a pinhead is a real story with real stakes, and he is the actual expert. That pillar turns him from "guy with a saw" into "the person who explains what's happening to the woods." That's what makes people want to be taught by him, and it's what a local news station eventually calls him about.

### His bio (do this on his phone today)

> **Appalachian Arborist** · [City], [State]
> The trees these mountains grow, and the ones they kill 🪓
> Steep ground. Hardwood. Storm work.
> DM **TIMBER** for my free tree health checklist 👇
> [link]

Name field on Instagram (the searchable one, not the handle): **Appalachian Arborist | Tree Removal [Region]**

His keyword is **TIMBER** so it doesn't collide with your CLIMB. His lead magnet is a one-page "5 signs your tree is about to fail" checklist. He can write it in 20 minutes and it's the single best lead magnet a tree company can have, because it gets shared by homeowners and it generates actual local estimate calls.

### His first 7 days

| Day | Post | On-screen hook |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Declaration | "I'm an arborist in the Appalachian mountains. I'm going to show you every tree these hills grow." |
| 2 | The Mountain | "This is what tree work looks like on a 40-degree slope." |
| 3 | The Species | "This hemlock is dying and it's being killed by something the size of a pinhead." |
| 4 | The Craft | "This is a $[X] removal. Here's why the access costs more than the tree." |
| 5 | The Storm | "Ice storm call, 6 AM, mountain road. This is the job nobody wants." |
| 6 | The Species | "5 signs your tree is about to come down. Number 3 is the one everybody misses." |
| 7 | The Why | "My family's been in these mountains [X] generations. Here's why I climb." |

Caption formula on every one: hook repeated, two lines of story, "Comment TIMBER and I'll send you my free tree health checklist," then #appalachia #arborist #treework #[hisstate] #treeremoval.

Day 6 is his best shot at a big one. A homeowner-useful list about trees failing gets shared into every neighborhood group in his county, and every share is a person who might need a tree removed.

### What he does Sunday
Screenshot his numbers, send them to you, and tell you which post did best and which did worst. That's it. That's the whole homework.

## 4 · Today's content with Ant

Two arborists at a kitchen table talking shop is better content than either of you alone. Shoot it with the actual cameras, cards are in now.

**Shot 1 · The session itself.** ZV-E10 on the tripod, wide, both of you at the table with notebooks and phones out. Let it roll the whole 90 minutes. You will pull three Reels out of it.

**Shot 2 · The one-take exchange.** Point the camera at Ant and ask him: *"What's the hardest tree you've ever taken down?"* Let him talk. Don't interrupt. That's a Reel on both accounts.

**Shot 3 · The before shot.** Film his phone screen showing his follower count today. Blur nothing, it's his own account and he agreed. That number on screen today is the payoff shot in 30 days.

**Shot 4 · The disagreement.** Ask him something you actually disagree about. Rigging technique, pricing, a species call. Two pros disagreeing respectfully is one of the highest-engagement formats there is and it's free.

**Shot 5 · The handshake at the truck.** 10 seconds, gimbal, him leaving. That's your closing shot for the 30-day case study film.

**Use these for:** a Reel this week ("I sat down with another tree guy and built his whole content plan in 90 minutes"), a collab post with Ant, and the raw material for a Crew Member of the Week when he has results.
