# The offer: Groundie

**Working name:** Groundie. The groundie is the guy on the ground who keeps the climber alive. Every tree guy knows the word, and you already use it in your hooks ("tag your groundie"). Change it if you want; the code does not care.

## One sentence

When a customer calls and you're on a job, Groundie texts them back in seconds, finds out what the tree needs, gets the address and photos, and hands you a quote-ready note. Emergencies page you now.

## Who buys it

Owner-operator tree companies first (you can speak to them as a peer, and you have a lead list). Then roofers, concrete, excavation, fence, welding: anyone whose business line is a cell phone in their pocket while their hands are busy. The ranker in `tools/leads` scores for exactly that.

## Why it is better than what they could buy today

- **GoHighLevel agencies** sell missed-call text-back at $297 to $497 a month. It sends a template and dumps the reply into an inbox the owner never opens. Groundie holds the conversation, asks for photos, and writes the crew note.
- **Podium** is $399 to $999 a month and built for dental offices with a front desk.
- **NextPhone / SalesCaptain** AI receptionists are $159 to $199 a month and generic. None of them know to ask whether the tree is over the house or near the lines.
- **MoeGo / Gingr** style vertical software charges $100 a month for texting as an add-on and still does not answer the phone.

Your edge is not the AI. It is that it asks the questions a climber would ask, and that you are a climber selling it to climbers on camera.

No contract. Cancel by text. Owners trust that more than any guarantee.

## The pitch, in your words

"I climb. I can't answer the phone 60 feet up, and 62% of calls to guys like us go unanswered. 85% of those people never call back. So I built a groundie for my phone. You call my line, I don't pick up, you get a text in ten seconds that asks what's going on with the tree and for a photo. By the time I'm on the ground I've got the address, the pictures, and a note I can quote from. I'll put it on your line this week. $197 a month, you keep your number, turn it off anytime."

## Objections

- **"My wife answers the phone."** Great, then it only fires when she can't. Ask how many calls hit voicemail last week.
- **"People hate texting bots."** It never says it's a bot unless asked, and the first text is "sorry we missed you, what's going on with your tree?" Show them the transcript from your own line.
- **"I'll just call them back."** 67% already dialed the next company by then. The text buys you the hour.
- **"$197 is a lot."** One trim job. Then stop talking.
- **"Can it quote?"** No, and that is on purpose. It gets you what you need to quote in three minutes from the truck.

## What they get in the install (90 minutes)

1. Toll-free number under your Twilio account, verification submitted (sends during review).
2. Profile JSON: services, area, hours, quote and deposit policy, their voice.
3. Conditional forwarding from their cell (no number change).
4. Owner alerts to their cell; the leads board link.
5. Three test calls with them watching. Film number three.
6. A one-page "how it works" text for their crew.

## What you measure (and post)

Calls caught, text-backs sent, replies, quote-ready leads, jobs won. The board at `/leads` has it. Screenshot it weekly for the client and for your content.
